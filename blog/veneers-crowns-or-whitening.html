Veneers explained: types, procedure, lifespan and alternatives

Porcelain or composite, how the treatment works, how long veneers last, and when whitening or crowns suit you better.

Many people contact us because they want a brighter or more even smile. Veneers are one way to get there, but not the only one. Which treatment fits depends on the health of your teeth as much as on how they look.

What are veneers?

Veneers are thin shells bonded to the front of a tooth. They change the colour and shape of the tooth and can close small gaps, so they are mostly used on the teeth that show when you smile. The aim is usually a natural, calm result that suits your face, rather than identical bright white teeth.

Porcelain or composite veneers?

Porcelain veneers are made by a dental laboratory. They keep their colour, resist stains and look much like natural enamel. Composite veneers are shaped directly on the tooth from a tooth-coloured resin, often in one visit. They cost less and usually need less of the tooth removed, but they stain and wear more easily.

Who are veneers suitable for?

Veneers usually suit healthy front teeth with healthy gums, when you would like to change their colour, shape or small gaps. They are less suitable if you have untreated decay or gum disease, if many of your teeth are heavily filled, or if you grind your teeth hard at night. In those cases your dentist may suggest treating that first, or a different solution.

How veneer treatment works

1. Consultation and plan. The dentist examines your teeth and gums, takes photos and scans, and discusses the shape and shade you would like. Many clinics show a mock-up of the new smile before anything is changed.

2. Preparation. For porcelain veneers, the dentist removes a thin layer of enamel, usually under local anaesthesia, and takes an impression or a digital scan. You wear temporary veneers while the laboratory works.

3. Laboratory work. A dental technician makes the veneers to match the agreed shape and shade. This often takes about a week.

4. Bonding and check. The dentist tries the veneers in, bonds them to the teeth and checks your bite. A short follow-up visit a few days later is common.

For porcelain veneers in Poland, many people plan one stay of about seven to ten days. The clinic confirms the exact schedule after the first examination.

How long do veneers last?

With good care, porcelain veneers often last ten to fifteen years or more, and composite veneers around five to seven years. Brush and clean between your teeth as usual, go for regular check-ups and avoid biting very hard objects. If you grind your teeth, a night guard helps protect the veneers.

Possible downsides

Removing enamel cannot be undone, so porcelain veneers are a lifelong commitment. Teeth can feel sensitive for a while after treatment. Veneers can chip or come loose, and they do not change colour with whitening, so any whitening should be done before the shade is chosen.

Alternatives to veneers

Whitening lightens the natural colour of your teeth with a bleaching gel, applied at the clinic or at home with trays your dentist gives you. It does not change the shape of your teeth or the colour of fillings, crowns or veneers. It is the least invasive option, and the effect fades gradually.

Crowns cover the whole visible part of a tooth. Dentists use them on teeth that are badly damaged or weakened by a large filling or root canal treatment, and on top of implants. A crown protects the tooth, but more of the tooth is removed than for a veneer.

Bonding and orthodontics. A small chip can often be fixed with composite bonding, and crooked teeth can sometimes be straightened with clear aligners instead of being covered.

Which one is right for me?

If your teeth are healthy and the colour is what bothers you, whitening is usually the first step. Veneers may suit healthy teeth whose shape or position you would like to change slightly. For damaged or heavily filled teeth, crowns tend to last longer. Many smile makeovers combine these treatments, with whitening first so that new veneers or crowns can be matched to the lighter shade.

Common questions

How long do veneers last?

With good care, porcelain veneers often last ten to fifteen years or more. Composite veneers usually need repairs or replacement sooner, often after around five to seven years.

Does getting veneers hurt?

The teeth are usually prepared under local anaesthesia, so the treatment itself should not hurt. Some people notice sensitivity to cold for a few days afterwards.

Can veneers be removed?

Porcelain veneers that required removing enamel are permanent: the tooth will always need a covering. Some composite veneers and minimal preparation veneers can be more reversible. Ask your dentist which applies to you.

How many veneers do I need?

It depends on how many teeth show when you smile. Some people need one or two, others six to ten on the upper jaw. Your dentist recommends the number after the examination.
