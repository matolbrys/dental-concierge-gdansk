Veneers, crowns or whitening: what is the difference?

Three treatments that change how your smile looks, but work in very different ways. A plain-language overview.

Many people contact us wanting a brighter or more even smile. The right treatment depends on the condition of your teeth, not only on how they look. Here is how the three most common options compare.

Whitening

Whitening lightens the natural colour of your teeth with a bleaching gel, either in the clinic or with trays at home under a dentist's supervision. It does not change the shape of the teeth, and it does not change the colour of existing fillings, crowns or veneers. It is the least invasive option, and the result fades gradually over time.

Veneers

Veneers are thin shells, usually made of porcelain, that are bonded to the front surface of a tooth. They can change colour, shape and small gaps or irregularities, and are mainly used on the teeth that show when you smile. In most cases a thin layer of enamel is removed to make room for the veneer, which means the treatment cannot be undone.

Crowns

A crown covers the whole visible part of a tooth. It is used when a tooth is heavily damaged, weakened by a large filling or root canal treatment, or to restore an implant. Crowns protect the tooth as well as improving its appearance, but more of the tooth is prepared than for a veneer.

Which one is right for me?

  • Healthy teeth, colour is the main concern: whitening is usually the first step.
  • Healthy teeth, but you want to change shape or alignment slightly: veneers may be an option.
  • Damaged or heavily restored teeth: crowns are often the more durable choice.

Many smile makeovers combine several of these. Whitening is often done first, so that new veneers or crowns can be matched to the lighter shade.

Before you travel, Nacre makes sure you have a written plan from the clinic that explains which treatment is proposed for which tooth, and why.

This article is general information and not medical advice. Only an examination by a dentist can show which treatment suits your teeth.
