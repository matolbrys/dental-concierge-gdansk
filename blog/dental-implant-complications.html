Dental implant complications: what can happen and how to reduce the risk

What is normal after implant surgery, which complications can happen, the warning signs, and how to reduce the risk.

Most implant treatments go well, but implant placement is surgery, and every surgery has risks. Knowing what is normal and what is not helps you choose a clinic carefully and act in time. This article is general information. Only a dentist can assess your own case.

What is normal after implant surgery

Swelling, tenderness and some bruising are common in the first days. Swelling often peaks after two to three days and then eases. Light bleeding on the first day is normal. Most people feel much better within about a week, especially if they follow the clinic's instructions on cooling, painkillers, food and cleaning.

Early complications

Infection. Signs include swelling or pain that increases after the third day, pus, a bad taste or fever. An infection is usually treated by the dentist, sometimes with antibiotics.

Nerve irritation. In the lower jaw, an implant placed close to a nerve can cause numbness or tingling in the lip, chin or tongue. It is often temporary, but it should be reported to the clinic straight away. A careful 3D scan before surgery is the main way to prevent it.

Sinus problems. In the upper jaw, the sinus lies close to the roots of the back teeth. If the bone is thin, the dentist may plan a sinus lift first. Tell the clinic if you notice a blocked nose or pressure on one side after surgery.

Implant not integrating. Sometimes the bone does not fuse with the implant, and it feels loose in the first months. The dentist usually removes it, lets the area heal and places a new implant later.

Long-term complications

Peri-implantitis. This is an inflammation of the gum and bone around an implant, similar to gum disease around a natural tooth. Bleeding or swollen gums around the implant are early signs. If left untreated, it can lead to bone loss and, in the end, loss of the implant. Good cleaning, regular check-ups and not smoking are the best protection.

Mechanical problems. A screw can come loose, or the crown can chip or crack, especially if you grind your teeth. These problems are usually repairable.

Gum recession. Over time the gum around an implant can recede, which can affect how it looks in the front of the mouth. Your dentist can tell you what can be done.

What increases the risk

Smoking, diabetes that is not well controlled, untreated gum disease, teeth grinding and poor cleaning all increase the risk of complications. Some medicines, for example certain treatments for osteoporosis, and earlier radiotherapy of the head or neck can also affect healing. Tell the clinic about your health and every medicine you take, so they can plan safely.

Warning signs that need a dentist

  • Swelling or pain that gets worse after the third day
  • Fever, pus or a persistent bad taste
  • Numbness that lasts after the anaesthesia has worn off
  • An implant or crown that feels loose
  • Bleeding that does not stop with gentle pressure

If swelling spreads towards the eye or neck, or you have difficulty swallowing or breathing, contact emergency services where you are straight away.

Common questions

How common are dental implant complications?

Serious complications are uncommon, and most implants heal well. Minor problems such as temporary swelling or a loose screw are more common and are usually easy to treat.

What is peri-implantitis?

An inflammation of the gum and bone around an implant, similar to gum disease around a natural tooth. Found early, it can often be stopped. Good cleaning, regular check-ups and not smoking help prevent it.

What happens if a dental implant fails?

The dentist usually removes the implant, lets the area heal and, in many cases, places a new one later. Ask before treatment what the guarantee covers in that situation.

What should I do if I have problems after returning home?

Contact the clinic, describe your symptoms and send photos. If the signs are urgent, seek emergency care where you are. Nacre helps you reach the clinic and arrange a follow-up.
