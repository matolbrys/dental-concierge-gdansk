Why implant treatment usually means two trips

From the first scan to the final crown: how a typical implant plan is structured, and why the healing time matters.

Every case is different and your dentist sets the final plan. Still, most implant treatments follow the same basic structure. Knowing it helps you plan time off work and travel.

Before you travel: records and a first assessment

Recent X-rays or photos can give the clinic a first impression and help estimate whether you are a good candidate. The final decision is made in person, after an examination.

First visit: examination, 3D scan and placement

The clinic examines your mouth and takes a 3D scan (CBCT) to measure the bone and plan the exact position of each implant. If everything is suitable, the implant can often be placed during the same stay, usually under local anaesthesia. You leave with a temporary solution if the gap is visible.

Healing: the implant bonds with the bone

After placement, the bone grows onto the surface of the implant. This process is called osseointegration and commonly takes around three to six months, sometimes longer if a bone graft was needed. During this time you are at home and live normally, with some care while eating.

Second visit: the final crown

Once the implant has healed, the clinic fits the abutment and takes an impression or digital scan. The final crown or bridge is made in a dental laboratory and fitted, often within a few days. This is the visit where you see the final result.

Are there faster options?

In some cases, a temporary tooth can be attached to the implant on the day of surgery. Whether this is possible depends on bone quality and the stability of the implant. It is a clinical decision that only your dentist can make after the scan.

After treatment

Implants need the same care as natural teeth: daily cleaning, including between the teeth, and regular check-ups. Your dentist at home can do routine check-ups if you bring a record of the implant system that was used.

Nacre plans both trips with you and coordinates with the clinic between visits, so you always know what comes next.

This article is general information and not medical advice. Timelines vary from patient to patient, and your treating dentist will confirm your plan.
