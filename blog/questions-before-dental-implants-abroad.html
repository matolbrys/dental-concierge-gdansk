Dental implants abroad: 8 questions to ask before you book

The right questions tell you more about a clinic than any price list. Here is what we ask on behalf of every patient.

An implant is a long-term decision. It is placed in your jawbone and is meant to last for many years, so the quality of planning and care matters far more than the headline price. These are the questions worth asking any clinic, at home or abroad.

1. Who will place the implant?

Ask for the name of the dentist or surgeon who will treat you, their qualifications and how often they place implants. Experience with your type of case, for example several missing teeth or a full arch, is more relevant than general experience.

2. Will I have a 3D scan before treatment?

A cone beam CT scan (CBCT) shows the volume and quality of the bone and the position of nerves and sinuses. It allows the implant position to be planned precisely. A plan made without a 3D scan should raise questions.

3. Which implant system do you use?

Ask for the manufacturer and the product line. Established systems are well documented and spare parts are widely available. Request a written record of the implant brand, type and size, so that a dentist at home can service it later.

4. What exactly is included in the price?

An implant is usually three parts: the implant itself, a connecting piece called an abutment, and the crown on top. Check whether the quote includes all three, as well as scans, temporary teeth, medication and follow-up visits.

5. How many visits will I need?

Many implant treatments need two visits a few months apart, because the implant needs time to heal into the bone before the final crown is fitted. Ask for a timeline in writing so you can plan your travel.

6. What if I need a bone graft?

If there is not enough bone, a graft or sinus lift may be needed first. This can add cost and healing time. A good clinic will tell you after the scan whether this is likely, not after you arrive.

7. What guarantee do you give?

Ask what the guarantee covers, for how long and under which conditions, for example regular check-ups and good oral hygiene. Get it in writing.

8. Who looks after me when I am back home?

Most follow-up questions come after you have travelled home. Agree in advance how you will reach the clinic, whether video consultations are possible and how a follow-up visit would be arranged.
